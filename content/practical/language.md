Chile has one of the most distinct dialects in Latin America. Many Spanish speakers do not easily understand Chileans, and Chileans do not always understand them either. In person, that usually becomes a bonding moment of teaching and roasting. But auto-translate is far less forgiving.

## The essentials

<div class="glossary">
<div class="glossary-entry">
  <span class="glossary-term">Taco</span>
  <span class="glossary-definition">Traffic jam</span>
  <span class="glossary-context">Nothing to do with food. "Mucho taco" = gridlock.</span>
</div>
<div class="glossary-entry">
  <span class="glossary-term">Cachai</span>
  <span class="glossary-definition">"You know?"</span>
  <span class="glossary-context">Chileans end every sentence with this.</span>
</div>
<div class="glossary-entry">
  <span class="glossary-term">Po</span>
  <span class="glossary-definition">Emphatic yes/no</span>
  <span class="glossary-context">"Sí po" = yeah. "No po" = nope. The most Chilean word.</span>
</div>
<div class="glossary-entry">
  <span class="glossary-term">Luca</span>
  <span class="glossary-definition">1,000 pesos</span>
  <span class="glossary-context">"Cinco lucas" = 5,000 CLP. Essential for markets.</span>
</div>
<div class="glossary-entry">
  <span class="glossary-term">Pololo/a</span>
  <span class="glossary-definition">Boyfriend / Girlfriend</span>
  <span class="glossary-context">NOT novio — that means fiancé in Chile.</span>
</div>
<div class="glossary-entry">
  <span class="glossary-term">Carrete</span>
  <span class="glossary-definition">Party, night out</span>
  <span class="glossary-context">"Vamos al carrete" = let's go out.</span>
</div>
<div class="glossary-entry">
  <span class="glossary-term">Fome</span>
  <span class="glossary-definition">Boring, lame</span>
  <span class="glossary-context">A "fome" restaurant is one to skip.</span>
</div>
<div class="glossary-entry">
  <span class="glossary-term">Bacán</span>
  <span class="glossary-definition">Awesome, cool</span>
  <span class="glossary-context">High praise.</span>
</div>
<div class="glossary-entry">
  <span class="glossary-term">Palta</span>
  <span class="glossary-definition">Avocado</span>
  <span class="glossary-context">NOT aguacate. You'll get a blank stare.</span>
</div>
<div class="glossary-entry">
  <span class="glossary-term">Once</span>
  <span class="glossary-definition">Afternoon tea / snack</span>
  <span class="glossary-context">Pronounced "OHN-seh." Light meal around 5–7pm.</span>
</div>
<div class="glossary-entry">
  <span class="glossary-term">Micro</span>
  <span class="glossary-definition">Public bus</span>
  <span class="glossary-context">Don't look for signs that say "bus."</span>
</div>
<div class="glossary-entry">
  <span class="glossary-term">Al tiro</span>
  <span class="glossary-definition">Right away</span>
  <span class="glossary-context">"Lo hago al tiro" = I'll do it right now.</span>
</div>
<div class="glossary-entry">
  <span class="glossary-term">Completo</span>
  <span class="glossary-definition">Loaded hot dog</span>
  <span class="glossary-context">Chile's iconic street food.</span>
</div>
<div class="glossary-entry">
  <span class="glossary-term">Guagua</span>
  <span class="glossary-definition">Baby</span>
  <span class="glossary-context">In the Caribbean this means "bus." In Chile, baby.</span>
</div>

## A warning about auto-translate

Speech to text often misses the cultural meaning, intention, and context behind Chilean Spanish. A Google review saying "Don't go on the weekends, too much taco" has nothing to do with food.
