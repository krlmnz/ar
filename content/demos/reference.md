You can cross Santiago in an evening and still not understand the country. The understanding starts when the city lets go of the road and the light changes temperature. Everything west of the hills is a negotiation with weather.

## The shelf

Between the cordillera and the water there is a strip of towns, vineyards, and roads that look shorter on a map than they feel in a car. People underestimate that strip. They book a lunch in the valley and a dinner on the coast and spend the hour between them apologizing.

Give the coast its own day. The drive is the point, and so is arriving while the boats are still out.

- Morning in the hills, if you must
- One stop, not three
- The water in time to walk before supper

## A rule you can keep

> The ocean is not a backdrop. If you can see it, stay long enough to feel the temperature change.

## What to carry back

Not a list of beaches. A sense of which way the country faces. Santiago looks at the mountains. The towns under them look at the water. A good trip lets you do both, on purpose, on different days.
