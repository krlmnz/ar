The southern terminus is the part that surprises people. Qhapaq Ñan is usually
told as a Peruvian story — Cusco, Machu Picchu, the Inca Trail — and the Chilean
end of it gets a sentence. But the road came all the way down here, and the
valley it stopped in is the one this guide is about.

**On the sources.** Length estimates for the full network vary widely, from
around 23,000 km to 60,000 km depending on what is counted as road; UNESCO's
figure of "more than 30,000 km" is the conservative one and the one used above.
The 40,000 km number that circulates most often is worth a second look — it is
frequently paired with the claim that the network is longer than the Earth's
circumference, which is about 40,075 km. It is not. It is roughly the same
length, which is remarkable enough without the flourish.
