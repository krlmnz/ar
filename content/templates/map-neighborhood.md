At neighbourhood scale the reader is deciding **what to do next**, not where to
go — so the pins carry category, not identity. Colour does the sorting and the
legend decodes it; the grouped list on the right repeats the same colours so the
two halves of the page read as one thing.

Keep the palette to five or six categories. Past that, hues start colliding and
the map turns into confetti.

**The place list has to actually be a neighbourhood.** The map frames whatever
you give it, so one out-of-town entry pulls the camera back to regional scale and
the template stops meaning anything. If a place needs a different frame, it
belongs on a different page.
